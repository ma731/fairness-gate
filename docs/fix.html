The fix

It can be closed. I did not close it.

Per-group thresholds take two thirds off the recall gap for four tenths of a point of accuracy. Here is the measurement, and here is why shipping it would have been the wrong call.

What it would take to fix

The gap can be closed. Here is the bill.

Proving a disparity exists is the easy half. Every point on the line is this same model under a different single threshold, and the shape is the argument: sliding one cut does not buy fairness, it buys a worse model that selects almost everybody. The lowest gap a single threshold reaches is 0.034, at a threshold of 0.05 and an accuracy of 0.602. That is not a fix, it is abandoning the model.

The honest accounting

Cheap in accuracy, expensive in everything else.

One threshold per group, chosen on the validation year to equalise recall, closes most of the gap: 0.312 falls to 0.114. Accuracy pays 0.0042 for it, which is four tenths of a point. By the only number most projects report, this is nearly free.

Two things that accounting hides, and both matter more than the accuracy.

The false positive gap goes the other way, from 0.145 to 0.196. Equalising one error rate across groups makes the other one less equal. This is not an implementation detail, it is the impossibility result arriving in person: with different base rates you are choosing which unfairness to keep, not removing unfairness.

And it needs the protected attribute at the moment of prediction. A system doing this has to look at someone's race to decide which threshold applies to them. In hiring, in lending, in most places anyone would actually want this, that is either unlawful or is itself the harm. A mitigation that only works by doing the thing you were trying to avoid belongs in the documentation as a rejected option with its reasoning attached, which is where this one is.

Thresholds are chosen on the validation year and applied unchanged to the test year, the same discipline as everything else here. Choosing them on the test year would be fitting the fix to the exam.

Conclusion

What I would tell a team about to ship this.

The gap is real, and it is not an artefact of the attributes

0.312 between the best and worst treated racial group, with a 95% interval that does not come near zero. Deleting race and sex from the features leaves 92% of it standing. You cannot make this go away by not looking.

Accuracy will tell you everything is fine

It sits between 0.77 and 0.84 across those same groups. A model can be equally accurate everywhere and still place its errors on the same people every time. If one number is all you check, this is invisible to you.

Audit where the attributes cross, not one at a time

False positive gap 0.145 by race, 0.080 by sex, 0.221 by the two together. Two acceptable averages can hide a much worse cell underneath them, and marginal audits are built to miss exactly that.

The fix that works may not be one you can use

Per-group thresholds cut the gap to 0.114 for 0.0042 of accuracy, and need the person's race at the moment of decision, and push the false positive gap up. There is no setting where everything is fair at once. You are choosing which unfairness to keep, so choose it out loud.

Put the limits somewhere a build can read them

The reason any of this is still true tomorrow is that the numbers are held against a declared file, regenerated on every commit, and checked monthly by a job nobody has to remember to run. A standard that lives in a document drifts. A standard that fails a build does not.

The honest move was never to find a model with no disparity. It was to measure the disparity, publish what closing it would cost, say which cost I was not willing to pay, and make it impossible to quietly change my mind later.
